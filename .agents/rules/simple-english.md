# Simple English & Clear Communication

## Core Rule
All communication, documentation, commit messages, code comments, and explanations must be written in **clear, simple, and plain English**. Break AI-generated defaults and write like a human.

---

## 1. The Vocabulary Blacklist
- **Banned Filler & Jargon**: Never use: `delve`, `robust`, `tapestry`, `mosaic`, `testament`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `harness`, `multifaceted`, `streamline`, `optimize`, `nestled`, `synergy`, `game-changer`, or `cutting-edge`.
- **Force Simple Verbs**:
  - Use "use" instead of "utilize".
  - Use "explore" or "check" instead of "delve".
  - Use "help" instead of "facilitate".
  - Use "start" instead of "commence".
  - Use "fix" or "solve" instead of "remediate".
  - Use "show" instead of "demonstrate".
- **Ban Exaggerated Adjectives**: Rely on concrete nouns and verbs rather than empty adjectives like *crucial, vital, profound, revolutionary,* or *seamless*.

---

## 2. Structural Constraints
- **Kill the Wrap-Up Paragraph**: Never include a summary or concluding paragraph. Stop writing immediately when all required information is delivered. Never use *"In conclusion," "Ultimately," "At the end of the day,"* or *"In summary."*
- **Ban Negative Parallelism**: Avoid defining things by what they are not (e.g., avoid *"It is not just X, but Y"* or *"More than merely A, it is B"*).
- **Break the Rule of Three**: Do not default to listing exactly three adjectives, three examples, or three bullet points everywhere. Vary sentence and list rhythm naturally.
- **Short Sentences & Active Voice**: Keep sentences under 15-20 words. Use active voice (*"The function validates the email"* instead of *"The email is validated by the function"*).

---

## 3. Tonal & Directness Directives
- **Eliminate Forced Neutrality**: Take a definitive stance. Do not validate both sides just to conclude that *"balance is key"* or *"it is a complex issue."*
- **Remove Moralizing & Preaching**: Deliver information directly without life advice or framing tasks as a *"journey,"* *"step forward,"* or *"opportunity for growth."*
- **Strip Meta-Language & Preamble**: Start directly with the answer. Never announce what you are going to do before doing it (no *"Here is a breakdown of..."* or *"Let's take a look at..."*).
- **No Physical Melodrama or Abstract Metaphors**: Describe physical reality, metrics, and domain logic literally without melodrama or mixed abstract senses.
